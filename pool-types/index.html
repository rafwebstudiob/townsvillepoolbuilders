Pool types in Townsville: concrete, fibreglass and plunge

In short: Most new backyard pools around Townsville are concrete, fibreglass or a smaller plunge pool. You don't need to have decided before you request a quote.

Here's a plain-English comparison, then a free form to pass your project to one local pool builder. We don't build pools ourselves.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

Concrete pools

A concrete (often sprayed or formed) pool is built on site, so the shape, depth and features can be tailored to the block. Raised spas, beach entries and freeform outlines are common reasons people choose concrete. The build usually takes longer than a standard fibreglass install because the shell is constructed, finished and cured on site. Site conditions — slope, access, soil and rock — still drive a large part of the quote.

The photo above and the one in the card on the homepage are stock images of inground pools, not a claim about a particular construction method on a Townsville site.

Fibreglass pools

A fibreglass pool arrives as a pre-formed shell and is craned or placed into an excavated hole, then packed, connected and finished. Time on site is often shorter than a full concrete build, provided the chosen shell fits the block and access is good enough to get the shell in. Sizes and shapes are limited to the moulds a manufacturer or dealer offers.

Plunge pools

A plunge pool is simply a smaller swimming pool — useful on compact courtyards, tighter suburban lots, or sloping sites where a full-length family pool would not fit. It can be concrete or fibreglass. Queensland pool safety laws still apply if it is a regulated pool (including many spas and some portable pools). Don't assume a small pool is exempt; check the official rules or ask the builder and certifier.

Choosing between them

There isn't a single “best” type. Fit to the block, the look you want, how long you can allow for construction, and what's included in the quote (shell, equipment, fencing, paving, landscaping) matter more than a generic comparison. If you're unsure, choose “Not sure” on the form and tell the builder what you want the pool to do.
