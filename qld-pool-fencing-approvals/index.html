QLD pool fencing and approvals in Townsville

In short: A new pool in Queensland needs building approval, a compliant safety barrier, certification before it is filled to 300 mm or more, and registration with the QBCC.

This guide summarises official QBCC, Queensland Government and local council rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

This page summarises official Queensland rules so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice, and it is not a substitute for a building certifier or a QBCC-licensed pool safety inspector. Rules and forms can change — follow the links and check with the certifier or your council if you're unsure.

The short answer

A new swimming pool in Queensland needs building development approval and must comply with the pool safety laws. The pool and its barrier must be inspected and certified before the pool is filled to a depth of 300 mm or more. Regulated pools must also be registered with the Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC). We don't arrange approvals ourselves; a local builder and a private building certifier usually handle that.

Building approval

The QBCC says that before building a pool you must get building approvals from your local council or a private certifier, and that in most cases the pool builder will arrange this. You or your builder need architectural plans and engineering specifications, and must engage a private certifier (who lodges the documents with the local council) or a certifier through the council. The certifier will say if you need a structural report on a difficult site, or extra approvals such as building over a sewer or town planning approval in a special zone.

Source: QBCC, Build a pool.

The Queensland Government also states that all new swimming pools need building development approval and must comply with the pool safety laws, and that you should contact a building certifier or your local council about approval. Source: qld.gov.au, Pool fencing and safety laws.

Townsville City Council

Townsville City Council says most building work needs a building permit, issued by a private building certifier, and lists swimming pools and spas, and their fences, as examples of work that need a permit. Planning approval from Council may also be required depending on the development. Rules for swimming pool fences are in Queensland Development Code Mandatory Part 3.4. Council says pool safety certificates must be obtained from a licensed pool safety inspector, and that Council does not employ licensed pool safety inspectors.

Sources: Townsville City Council, Building and renovating; Townsville City Council, Swimming pools and spas.

Filling the pool and temporary fencing

The building certifier who approved the pool must inspect and certify the pool fence before the pool is filled to a depth of 300 mm or more. If it is approved, the certifier issues a Form 17 (final inspection certificate for pools and barriers) or, where it applies, a Form 11 (certificate of occupancy for a building with a regulated pool), and must provide a copy to the QBCC.

A temporary fence may be used instead of a permanent fence for up to three months only if it has been inspected and approved by the same building certifier, who should issue a Form 16. It can only be used longer than three months with further written approval from the certifier. A permanent compliant fence is required before the certifier can give the final inspection certificate.

Source: QBCC, Build a pool.

The pool safety standard (barriers and fences)

The current standard for swimming pool barriers in Queensland is Queensland Development Code Mandatory Part 3.4 (QDC MP 3.4). Cairns Regional Council also points to Australian Standards AS 1926.1 and AS 1926.2 alongside QDC MP 3.4.

According to the QBCC's pool fences page, fences must be:

  • at least 1,200 mm high, measured from finished ground level to the top of the fence
  • with a maximum gap of 100 mm from finished ground level to the bottom of the barrier
  • built on stable and solid ground
  • a minimum of 1,200 mm high at any location on sloping or stepped sites, measured at a right angle to the ground

There must be a non-climbable zone of at least 900 mm between rails (measured from the top of one rail to the top of the rail below). The QBCC also sets out how closer rails, taller fences and objects near the fence are treated. The standard covers gates and latching, and preventing direct access from a building into the pool area. Homeowners must keep the barrier compliant at all times, including by removing climbable objects such as furniture near the fence.

If a boundary fence or the wall of a building forms part of the barrier, that part still has to meet the standard. If it doesn't, a separate barrier inside the property may be needed.

Registering the pool

All regulated pools must be registered with the QBCC. Registration can be done through the QBCC pool register. The Queensland Government says you will be fined if your pool isn't registered. The QBCC also notes that it audits properties and that failing to register can lead to a fine.

Under the Building Act 1975, a swimming pool is taken to include a spa. Bathroom spas are not considered swimming pools unless they are continuously filled to a depth above 300 mm. Some portable pools and spas also have to comply — the Queensland Government sets tests based on depth, volume and whether there is a filtration system. Check the official page if you are considering a portable pool.

Pool safety certificates (Form 23)

A pool safety certificate (Form 23) confirms that a pool complied with the pool safety standard at the time a QBCC-licensed pool safety inspector checked it. Only a pool safety inspector licensed by the QBCC can issue a Form 23. Certificates are valid for one year for shared pools and two years for non-shared pools.

You need a Form 23 if you are selling, buying or leasing a property with a regulated pool. You may also get one for peace of mind. If a pool or fence has recently been certified by a building certifier, a Form 17 (or, for some new pools, a Form 11) can replace a Form 23 for the same period.

If the inspector is not satisfied the pool complies, they issue a Form 26 non-conformity notice (unless a quick reinspection or agreed minor repairs fall within the rules). You must have the pool re-inspected within three months by the same inspector; otherwise the inspector must tell the local council, and fines can apply. You cannot simply switch inspectors within that period to get a certificate, except in limited circumstances through the QBCC.

Owners of shared pools must display the current certificate near the main entrance or at a gate or door to the pool. Owners of non-shared pools do not have to display it.

Sources: QBCC, Inspect and certify a pool; QBCC, Guide to pool safety for homeowners (PDF).

Contracts, deposits and checking a licence

The QBCC advises you to check the pool builder's licence and licence history before you accept a quote or sign a contract. For domestic building work over $3,300 you need a written contract, a five-business-day cooling-off period, and a QBCC-approved Contract Information Statement. The deposit must not be more than 10% for work up to $20,000, or 5% for projects of $20,000 or more. The Queensland Home Warranty Scheme premium is compulsory for those projects and is usually collected with the deposit. Confirm in the quote what is included — for example, whether fencing is in the price.

Source: QBCC, Build a pool.

During construction

The QBCC requires a weatherproof warning sign before construction starts, warning that a swimming pool is under construction and of the danger to young children, placed within 1.5 m of the road frontage, visible from the road, with the bottom of the sign at least 300 mm above ground, in bold text at least 50 mm high. A sign is only required on one road frontage.

Source: QBCC, Build a pool.

Where to get official help

The QBCC can be contacted on 13 93 33. Licensed pool safety inspectors and the pool register are on the QBCC website. For local building certification questions, use the council links above or a private certifier registered with the QBCC.
