Building a pool near sewer or stormwater pipes in Townsville

In short: Townsville City Council will not allow building over or near water supply mains, sewer pressure mains, gravity trunk sewers or sewer connection points. Next to an ordinary gravity sewer main, the Townsville City Plan sets a minimum clearance for pools of 1 m horizontally and 600 mm vertically from the edge of the pipe. Work that does not meet the state code needs Council consent as a referral agency.

Underground pipes often decide where a pool can go in a backyard. This guide summarises Townsville City Council and Queensland Government rules, with links to the source documents. It is not engineering or legal advice.

Last updated: 26 November 2026

This page summarises official documents so you know what to raise with your builder and certifier. It is not engineering, legal or planning advice. Your builder, a private building certifier or Townsville City Council can confirm what applies to your lot.

Why underground pipes matter for a pool

An inground pool is a large excavation. Many Townsville blocks have a council sewer main, a house connection point or a stormwater pipe running through the backyard or along a boundary. The Townsville City Plan says that any structure built over or near a council service must not affect how the service works, must not put extra load on it without Council approval, and must not interfere with maintenance access, which must be available at all times.

Townsville Water, part of Townsville City Council, owns and maintains the sewerage network up to and including the riser (sometimes called the jump-up). The property owner owns the house drain from the home to that point. Council notes that the property connection point is usually inside the property boundary, but can be outside it and even some distance away. Source: Townsville City Council, Common wastewater questions.

The state rule: QDC MP 1.4

In Queensland, building work over or near sewers, water mains, stormwater drains and combined sanitary drains is assessed against the Queensland Development Code MP 1.4, Building over or near relevant infrastructure. Business Queensland says MP 1.4 applies when the lot contains relevant infrastructure, is next to a lot or road reserve that does, or has an easement registered by a service provider. It does not apply when the work is far enough away, which includes being 3 m away for a Class 1 or Class 10 building or structure. Under the National Construction Code, a swimming pool is a Class 10 structure.

If the work does not comply with an acceptable solution in MP 1.4, the application must be referred to the service provider as a referral agency. For council sewers and stormwater in Townsville, that is Townsville City Council. Business Queensland says referral agencies have 20 business days to assess an application and may charge a fee.

One of the MP 1.4 acceptable solutions, for sewers up to 225 mm and stormwater drains up to 375 mm that are not pressure pipelines, is that excavation must not be carried out within 600 mm of the outer wall of the pipe.

Sources: Business Queensland, Build over infrastructure; QDC MP 1.4 (PDF).

What Townsville City Council won't allow

The Townsville City Plan's design standards for building over or next to services (section SC6.4.3.2) say that, without exception, Council will not permit a structure to be built over or near:

  • sewer pressure mains and overflow pipework
  • gravity sewer trunk mains
  • sanitary drainage connection points
  • water supply mains
  • stormwater pipes or culverts with a capacity of less than 2% annual exceedance probability (AEP), or with an internal diameter under 375 mm
  • open drainage channels or overland flow paths.

Pools next to an ordinary gravity sewer main

Council may allow building over or near a gravity sewer main that is not a trunk main. The applicant has to arrange a CCTV inspection inside the main and give it to Council before construction starts, and another inspection afterwards to confirm the work has not damaged the sewer. If the main is in poor condition or likely to be damaged, the applicant may have to replace it first.

The clearances in the City Plan that are most relevant to a backyard pool are:

  • Pools: at least 600 mm vertically and 1 m horizontally from the outer edge of the service footprint
  • Connection points: at least 2.0 m of clear horizontal space between a structure and a sewer connection point (an oblique branch, a vertical jump-up or the centre of a maintenance structure)
  • Manholes: at least 2.4 m of clear vertical space above a sewer manhole, measured from the finished ground surface
  • Access: an unobstructed path at least 1 m wide with 2.4 m clear headroom to sewer maintenance structures, and a maintenance vehicle must be able to park within 50 m of them.

If there is no other reasonable place for the structure, or it would conflict with or damage a council service, Council can require the service to be relocated at the developer's expense.

Source: Townsville City Plan, SC6.4.3 Design standards (PDF, version 2022/01). Check the current Townsville City Plan, or ask Council, in case it has been updated.

How to find out where your pipes are

  • As constructed drainage plan: Townsville City Council can supply a plan of the sanitary drainage from its sewer connection to the house, usually with measurements and depths. A search fee applies, and it is not refunded if no plan exists. These plans do not show stormwater or water service pipes, and some older properties have no plan on record. Request it through Council's as constructed drainage plan request.
  • Before You Dig Australia: BYDA offers free plans of underground and overhead infrastructure from the asset owners for your site.
  • On site: look for inspection openings and maintenance holes in the yard, and ask the builder to mark the pipes on the site plan before the design is finalised.

Who applies, and how

If a design needs Council's consent, it is lodged as a Referral Agency Response Application, which Council says is often called a "building over services request". The fee is set in Council's Planning and Development Schedule of Fees and Charges. Building approval for the pool itself comes from a private building certifier, and our QLD pool fencing and approvals guide covers that side.

Questions to ask on the builder's site visit

  • Where do the sewer, stormwater and water lines run on my block, and how did you confirm it?
  • Does the pool position meet the MP 1.4 acceptable solutions, or will it need a Council referral?
  • If a referral or CCTV inspection is needed, who arranges it, and is the cost in the quote?
  • Could moving or resizing the pool avoid the pipe altogether?
  • Is the quote conditional on the approvals, and what happens to the price if the pool has to move?
